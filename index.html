Amp Football Coach Board v0 prototype for coach feedback

Standard crutch-based amputee football: 7v7 on a 60 x 40 m pitch. Plays are stored only on this device. The page sends nothing; a play leaves your device only if you export a file and choose to send it.

Frame 1 / 1

Arrows drawn in a frame show the movement that leads to the next frame. Yellow solid = run, white dashed = pass. Tokens: blue = your team (GK = goalkeeper, 1 to 6 = outfield players, role labels only, no names), orange = opponent, white = ball. The starting spread is not a recommended formation.

How to use this board (about 5 minutes)
  1. Move players. With Move selected, drag a token. Blue is your team (GK, then 1 to 6), orange is the opponent, white is the ball. Untick Show opponent to hide the orange team.
  2. Draw arrows. Select Run arrow (yellow, a player's movement) or Pass arrow (white dashed), then drag on the pitch from start to end. Undo last arrow removes the newest one and Clear arrows removes all arrows in the current frame. Select Move again to drag tokens.
  3. Build a sequence. A frame is one picture of the play. Press Add frame (copy), then drag the tokens to where they end up. Arrows in a frame show the movement that leads to the next frame. Use Prev and Next to step through, Play to watch it animate, and Loop to repeat it.
  4. Fill in the play card. Give the play a name, choose the situation, write per-role notes, and set the source and review status. Press Save to library.
  5. Use the library. Saved plays are grouped by situation. Load opens one and Delete removes it. The library lives only in this browser on this device.
  6. Share. Export library (JSON) saves all your plays to a file you can send. Import JSON loads a file you received. Export PNG saves a picture of the frame you are looking at.

Use role labels only, no player names.

Rules reference (unverified summary, check the current WAFF Laws)
Summarised from two WAFF Laws of the Game PDFs by an automated reader, not checked against the PDFs by a person. The two versions disagree on some numbers (listed below). Confirm which version your competition uses.
  • Pitch 60 m x 40 m, plus or minus 5 m depending on venue. Centre circle radius 6 m. Goal area 10 m wide x 8 m deep. Penalty mark 8 m from the goal line. Goal 5 m wide x 2.2 m high. The penalty area is mentioned in Law 9 but its size is not given in the text, so it is not drawn here (Laws 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 2.2).
  • 7 players per side including the goalkeeper. A match may not start with fewer than 5 outfield players, a goalkeeper and a reserve goalkeeper (Law 3).
  • Two periods of 25 minutes, one time-out per team per half of at most 1 minute, half-time at most 10 minutes (Law 6.1).
  • Substitutions only at a stoppage with the referee's permission, at most 2 players at a time, substituted players may return (Law 3.6).
  • Versions differ: substitutes named 5 (2017 version) or 6 (later upload); squad 15 on the roster with 12 dressed (2017) or 13 maximum (later upload). England domestic rules list a squad of 10 maximum. Enter your own competition's numbers.
  • Crutches are treated as extensions of the arms. Intentionally using a crutch to control or direct the ball is a handling violation (direct free kick). Defender in own penalty area: penalty kick. Attacker in the penalty area: defending side takes a direct free kick from anywhere in the goal area (Laws 9.1.1 to 9.1.3).
  • No offside in amputee football (England Football and Wikipedia rule summaries).

Play card

Anything involving player load, fatigue or crutch strain needs a physiotherapist, not a coach, to review it.

Library (this device only)

Clearing browser data deletes the library. Export a copy to share plays or keep a backup.

Share feedback or plays

  1. Press Save to library for each play you want to share.
  2. Press Export library (JSON), then send the file to , with a few lines on what was missing or confusing.

Use role labels only. Do not put player names, injuries or private club details in the notes. Nothing you send is published without asking you first.
